What this board is

S8 + S9 — two ways to move through a long session. Find (⌘F) docks a bar under the window head, searches the whole history (not just what’s loaded), lists matches with context, and jumps — loading older turns and opening folds as needed. The trail is a thin rail on the left edge with one tick per message you sent; hover shows what you asked and what came back, click jumps there. Neither ever moves your reading position on its own.

05

The trail — one tick per message you sent, a card on hover

US-029.AC-1 · task_08 VC-04
A 28px rail on the scroller’s left edge, ticks 2px tall. Rest ticks sit at 20% opacity; the ones for messages currently in view at 52%; the reading anchor (the last sent message at or above the top of the viewport) at 90% and wider; the one under your pointer at 100%. Width breathes toward the pointer like a dock — color never moves. The card is the outline entry: what you asked, then the final reply that turn produced. The rail appears at ≥864px pane width with two or more sent messages; below that it’s absent, not squeezed.

06

Sixty messages — the trail compresses instead of becoming a wall

US-029.EC-1 · task_08 VC-05
Layout depends only on the count. Spacing shrinks from 9px to 3px as the count grows, capped at 80% of the pane height; beyond what fits, the ticks are distributed proportionally (every tick still has its own hit target, 8px tall). Magnification widens to a Gaussian window so a dense rail is still pickable. No ResizeObserver, no measurement loop.

07

Click jumps — respecting who owns the scroll · a new turn adds a tick without a shift

US-029.EC-2 · US-029.AC-2
A jump is a manual navigation: the scroller goes to free mode and stays there. The target bubble lands 16px under the top; the tick becomes the anchor. While streaming, a new sent message appends a tick at the end of the rail; existing ticks keep their positions (spacing only recomputes when the count crosses a threshold, and then all at once, in a 90ms transition — none under reduced motion).
