# Blog SEO audit and corrections

## Scope and outcome

SEO follow-up to the [editorial rework](2026-09-11-blog-rework.md), September 11, 2026.
The scope is the six revised articles and the blog metadata/discovery surfaces that serve them.
The two older article bodies are unchanged. Changes are local; no deployment or Search Console
submission has been performed. This report does not claim rankings, search volume, or traffic gains.

The main problems were intent mismatches in three URLs, inconsistent article identity across
metadata, limited contextual links, and empty category archives in the sitemap. The corrections
align discoverability with the practical work each article actually delivers. Runnable examples,
explicit evidence limits, original sources, and substantive explanations remain in the posts.

## Basis for the decisions

- Google recommends descriptive, concise, distinct titles and discourages keyword stuffing.
  It specifies no fixed title-character limit; truncation depends on display width.
  [Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link).
- Article metadata can identify the author with a profile URL and provide publication/modification
  dates. The existing author record supplies the identity; no credentials were invented.
  [Article structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article).
- Sitemap modification times should reflect a significant content update. Build timestamps are
  unsuitable substitutes. [Sitemap guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap).
- URL moves use permanent server redirects to relevant final destinations, with internal links
  and canonical declarations updated together.
  [URL migration guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes).

## Remaining measurement boundary

This audit can verify local rendering, crawl directives, metadata, links, assets, and redirects.
Search Console coverage, field Core Web Vitals, actual queries/click-through rates, and search
positions require the deployed pages and account data. No field-performance score or indexing
success is inferred from a local build. After publication, the useful next measurement is coverage
of the six canonical URLs and the queries that bring readers to each practical guide.
